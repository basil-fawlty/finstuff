# Regime C: קופ״ג 190 (Tikun 190) — Tax Rules, Assumptions, and Conclusions

## What Is Tikun 190?

תיקון 190 (Amendment 190 to the Income Tax Ordinance) is a provision that allows individuals aged 60+ to deposit lump-sum savings into a קופת גמל (provident fund) and benefit from a **reduced capital gains tax rate** on withdrawals taken as a monthly annuity (קצבה). It is the primary vehicle for retirees to shelter accumulated non-pension savings from the standard 25% capital gains tax.

---

## Tax Rules

### Outside the Wrapper (Baseline)

The baseline depends on the fund type holding the outside investment:

| Outside fund type | Tax base | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign UCITS ETF in taxable account | Nominal gain (`FV − cost`) | 25% |
| Israeli קרן מחקה (qualifying tracker) | Real gain (`FV − cost × (1+i)^n`) | 25% |

The comparison is switchable in the calculator (nominal vs. real gains outside).

### Inside the Wrapper (Regime C)

- **Tax rate**: reduced statutory rate τ_B, typically **15%** on the nominal gain at withdrawal
- **Tax base**: nominal gain = `withdrawal proceeds − original deposit` (pro-rata)
  - The wrapper is treated as a single pool by the tax authority
  - Internal portfolio turnover **does not trigger a tax event**
  - Internal turnover gains are **deferred**, not eliminated — they accumulate into the balance and are taxed at τ_B when eventually withdrawn or liquidated
  - Cost basis = original deposit, unchanged by any internal trades or rebalancing
- **When tax is collected**: on each withdrawal (pro-rata gain portion) and on terminal liquidation
- **Inflation adjustment**: none — Regime C taxes the full nominal gain, not the real gain
- **Turnover shield**: a key structural advantage — outside, each portfolio rebalance or fund switch realises a taxable gain immediately; inside, the same rebalance is costless

### Key Structural Difference from Regime A and Regime B

| Feature | Regime A (real-gains) | Regime B (reduced rate, nominal) | Regime C (Tikun 190) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tax base | Real gain (inflation-adjusted) | Nominal gain | Nominal gain |
| Tax rate | 25% | τ_B < 25% | τ_B (typically 15%) |
| Turnover tax | Applies outside | Applies outside | **Deferred inside, never triggered mid-hold** |
| Inflation sensitivity | High | None | None |
| Combined benefit | Inflation shield | Rate reduction | Rate reduction + turnover deferral |

Regime C is Regime B with the additional turnover-deferral feature made explicit.

| Withdrawal mode | Inflation-adjusted | Withdrawals grow at i%/yr; real spending constant |

### Turnover Mechanics in Detail

**Outside the wrapper:**
Each year, `turnover%` of the balance is treated as realised:
1. Tax is paid on the gain portion of that slice: `tax = (balance − basis) × turnover × τ_out`
2. The after-tax proceeds are immediately reinvested
3. Basis steps up for the turned-over slice to current market value (post-tax reinvestment cost)
4. Net effect: current tax leak + higher future basis → lower future tax

**Inside the wrapper:**
Turnover is entirely internal — no tax event, no basis step-up, no cash leak. The full pre-tax gain stays invested and compounds. At withdrawal or liquidation, the entire cumulative gain (including all gains generated by internal turnover) is taxed at τ_B on the nominal amount.

This is **tax deferral with a rate reduction**, not tax elimination. The deferred turnover gains will be taxed eventually, but at 15% rather than 25%, and after benefiting from additional years of compounding.

## Conclusions

### 1. The wrapper advantage has three additive sources

| Source | Mechanism | Typical magnitude (20yr, 10% turnover) |
|---|---|---|
| Rate reduction | 25% → 15% on all gains | Large — dominant benefit |
| Turnover deferral | Turnover tax deferred until withdrawal | Moderate — grows with turnover% |
| Fee drag | Wrapper costs R−f to grow | Negative — partially offsets above |

### 2. Fee tolerance is high

Unlike Regime A (real-gains shield only, ~0.15–0.25% fee ceiling), Regime C tolerates significantly higher fees because the rate reduction is a 10 percentage point discount on the full nominal gain — a much larger benefit at realistic return and horizon combinations. The break-even fee at 20–25 year horizons is approximately **0.6–0.7%/yr** before counting turnover benefit. Turnover pushes break-even higher still.

### 3. Turnover materially increases the advantage

At 0% turnover, Regime C is pure Regime B (rate reduction only). Each additional 10% of annual turnover adds to the outside tax burden while leaving the inside unaffected. At 50%+ turnover (e.g., actively managed fund outside vs. wrapper), the turnover component rivals the rate-reduction component in magnitude.

### 4. The outside comparison matters

Comparing Regime C against a **real-gains קרן מחקה** (25% on real gain, low turnover) produces a much narrower advantage than comparing against a **nominal-gains UCITS ETF** (25% on nominal gain, higher turnover). The correct baseline is the actual fund the investor would hold outside the wrapper.

### 5. Inflation is not irrelevant — but only through the outside comparison

When the outside fund uses real-gains taxation, higher inflation *reduces* the outside tax burden, narrowing the Regime C advantage. When the outside fund uses nominal taxation, inflation is irrelevant to the comparison (both sides tax nominal gain). Inflation does reduce the real value of the deferred tax liability inside the wrapper — a second-order benefit not captured in the base model.

### 6. Revised decision rule for Tikun 190

- **Below 0.5% extra fee**: Regime C is a clear win at all horizons beyond ~5 years, at any realistic turnover level.
- **0.5%–0.7% extra fee**: Defensible for horizons under 20 years, especially with turnover > 10%.
- **Above 0.7% extra fee**: Requires either lower τ_B than 15%, additional benefits (e.g., employer match, contribution deduction), or high turnover outside to justify.
- **Zero-fee wrapper**: The full rate-reduction benefit is captured; turnover deferral is pure upside. Seek providers charging < 0.3% on balance.

## Inheritance Rules (פטור בירושה)

### Inside the Wrapper — Tikun 190

The inheritance treatment depends on the **age of the account holder at death** and the **heir's choice** of how to receive the funds.

**Death before age 75 — full exemption:**
Heirs may withdraw the entire accumulated balance (principal + all gains) fully tax-free. Alternatively, they may transfer the balance to a provident fund account in their own name without triggering a tax event — the tax event is deferred to the day they actually withdraw.

**Death at age 75 or later — reduced rate:**
Heirs may withdraw as a lump sum at **15% on the nominal gain** (the same rate as the original account holder). Alternatively, heirs aged 60+ may convert the balance to a monthly annuity, which is **tax-free for life**. The heir does not need to prove minimum pension income — the minimum-pension condition applied only to the original depositor.

**The 90-day rule:**
In both cases, the balance passes to heirs with full tax exemption (on all gains accumulated up to the date of death) for the first **90 days after death**. Any gains that accumulate after the 90-day window are subject to 25% tax on real gains. Heirs should act quickly — transfer or withdraw within 90 days to preserve the favourable tax treatment on the full accumulated gain.

**Transfer without tax event:**
At any age of the deceased, heirs can transfer the funds to a new provident fund account in their own name without that transfer being treated as a tax event. Tax is deferred until the heir eventually withdraws.

### Outside the Wrapper — Taxable Account

Israeli tax law does **not** provide a step-up in cost basis at death for taxable investment accounts (unlike US law). Death is **not** a taxable event for the outside account — no tax is triggered when the position passes to the heir. However, the heir also receives **no basis reset**: they inherit the original purchase price as their cost basis, and will owe capital gains tax on the full accumulated gain (from the original purchase date) when they eventually sell.

This has an important implication for the estate comparison:

- **Wrapper (death before 75, or heir transfers to deferred account):** terminal tax = 0 on transfer. The tax clock resets only if the heir later withdraws cash.
- **Wrapper (death at 75+, heir withdraws cash):** terminal tax = 15% on nominal gain at withdrawal.
- **Outside account:** no tax at death, but the heir inherits the full unrealised gain at the original cost basis. The 25% tax on real gains is merely **deferred**, not forgiven.

The true estate advantage of the wrapper is therefore not a simple comparison of "wrapper pays 0%, outside pays 25% now." The correct framing is: the wrapper **eliminates the liability** (before 75) or reduces it to 15% nominal (after 75), while the outside account **defers** a 25% real-gains liability to the heir. The present value of that deferred outside liability depends on when the heir eventually sells — if they hold for many more decades, the outside deferral has real value too.

| Scenario | Wrapper terminal tax | Outside terminal tax |
|---|---|---|
| Death before 75, heir withdraws cash | 0% | 0% now — but heir owes 25% real when they sell |
| Death before 75, heir transfers to deferred account | 0% (deferred further) | 0% now — but heir owes 25% real when they sell |
| Death at 75+, heir withdraws cash | 15% nominal | 0% now — heir owes 25% real when they sell |
| Death at 75+, heir converts to annuity (age 60+) | 0% (annuity tax-free) | 0% now — heir owes 25% real when they sell |

### Estate Model in the Calculator

The calculator models the estate scenario as follows:

- **Wrapper terminal tax**: 0 if estate-exempt (death before 75, or heir transfers), 15% nominal otherwise.
- **Outside terminal tax in estate mode**: 0 — no tax event at death. The heir inherits the position at original cost basis; the deferred 25% liability is not modelled as a current cash outflow, because its present value depends on the heir's future holding period and discount rate.
- **Advantage interpretation**: in estate mode, the wrapper advantage shown is the **immediate cash advantage** — what the heir receives today vs. what the outside heir receives today. The outside heir's position carries an embedded future tax liability not reflected in the current balance figure.
